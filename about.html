RangeGuessr

About RangeGuessr

RangeGuessr is a daily wildlife geography game. Each day you're shown an animal, and your job is twofold: place a pin where you think it lives in the wild, and guess its conservation status. The closer your pin lands to the species' actual range — and the closer your status guess is to its real IUCN listing — the more points you score, up to 10,000.

How to play

You get one animal per day in Daily mode. Study the photo and hint, click the map to drop your guess, and pick a conservation status from Extinct through Least Concern (plus Data Deficient for species science hasn't fully assessed). Submit to reveal the animal's true range, its status, and a fact about it. Come back each day to keep your streak alive, or switch to Free Play for unlimited rounds that don't affect your streak.

Scoring

Location is worth up to 5,000 points: a pin inside the animal's range scores full marks, and points fall off with distance from the nearest edge of its range. Conservation status is worth another 5,000: an exact match is full marks, and you lose points the further your guess sits from the real listing on the threat scale. Data Deficient species score on a flat scale, since "we don't have enough data" isn't a point on that spectrum.

Why conservation?

Most geography games are pure trivia. RangeGuessr exists partly because a lot of remarkable species are quietly disappearing, and most people have never heard of them. Every round is a small chance to learn that an animal exists, where it clings on in the wild, and how close to the edge it really is. If a single saola or vaquita sticks in your memory after playing, the game has done its job.

Where the data comes from

Species occurrence data and photos are drawn from GBIF (the Global Biodiversity Information Facility), used under CC BY 4.0. Conservation statuses follow the IUCN Red List categories. Base map boundaries come from Natural Earth. Range polygons are simplified for gameplay and are approximate — they're meant to teach the broad strokes of where a species lives, not to serve as a scientific range map.
